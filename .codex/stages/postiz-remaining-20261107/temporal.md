Cancellation is propagated by the new workflow rather than swallowed by auxiliary error handling.
- Every activity publication attempt emits an immediate heartbeat and repeats calls every 15 seconds. Details are strictly `{ version: 'publication-heartbeat/v1', stage }`, where stage is one of `inspect`, `preparing`, `claiming`, `submitting`, `confirming`, `confirmed`. No content, settings, credentials, provider payload or unbounded error enters the diagnostic. The timer is cleared in `finally`; SDK network throttling can make actual server heartbeat delivery less frequent than the local 15-second call interval.
- Cancellation checks occur before preparation/claim and immediately before provider submission. Cancellation before claim leaves the ledger eligible. Once a claim exists, cancellation or lost provider response retains uncertainty and never permits automatic resubmission. After a provider returns a receipt, no cancellation check or cancellation-bound operation runs before atomic confirmation. Heartbeat diagnostic errors are contained, including at the `confirming` transition. Confirmation precedes campaign projection, streak, notifications, webhook and plug actions. A hard process loss before the receipt reaches the database can still leave an uncertain claim; recovery blocks a duplicate rather than inventing confirmation.
- NEW `apps/orchestrator/src/workflows/missing.post.workflow.v111.ts`: scans hourly, rotates history after 100 turns and calls the V111 recovery activity. Recovery uses `signalWithStart('postWorkflowV110', USE_EXISTING)` for both ordinary and campaign roots. A running `post_<id>` job is poked and keeps its existing workflow type; a new root starts V110. No POST job is terminated or migrated by this scanner.
- Registration: `apps/orchestrator/src/app.module.ts` and `workflows/index.ts` retain all older registrations and add only V110/V111. `PostsService.startWorkflow` now selects V110 for every new ordinary/campaign UI start; its existing explicit edit/reschedule stop behavior is unchanged.

## Recovery root migration policy

`InfiniteWorkflowRegister` accepts only `RUN_CRON=true` or `RUN_CRON=1`; false/0/empty/unset performs no SDK calls. Its bootstrap now starts or reuses `missing-post-workflow-v111` / `missingPostWorkflowV111` first. Only after that succeeds does it retire the exact control-plane IDs `missing-post-workflow-v108` and `missing-post-workflow-v110`. It never obtains a handle for a `post_` job or repeat child. If the successor cannot start, no old root is retired. If retirement fails, bootstrap reports `PUBLICATION_RECOVERY_MIGRATION_FAILED`; the successfully started successor remains safe, and retry uses `USE_EXISTING`.

This is source configuration, not a performed live migration. A separately authorized rollout must retain all old workflow/activity versions, inspect active history/worker compatibility and confirm the new root is active. Historical generic/v106/v107 roots are outside this exact automatic retirement whitelist and require explicit control-plane inventory before rollout. Existing POST jobs and their repeats finish under their frozen versions. Rollback must preserve V110/V111 workers for their histories and preserve every V106 publication journal/pending claim; never clear a claim or send through a legacy writer to bypass uncertainty. `docs/operations/publication-recovery-v106.md` was reconciled to the V110/V111 source consumer/control-plane policy while preserving historical contracts and explicit rollout boundaries.
