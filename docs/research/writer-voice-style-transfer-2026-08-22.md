# Extracting a Writer's Voice and Transferring Style Without Identity: A Research Report

## TL;DR
- **Feature 1 ("My voice") is well-grounded and buildable now**: a hybrid pipeline — deterministic stylometric metrics computed first (function-word/particle frequencies, punctuation, sentence-length distribution, POS ratios), then handed to an LLM as evidence in a map-reduce architecture — is the defensible design. Established science says function words/particles, punctuation and character n-grams are the reliable discriminators; vocabulary-richness measures (Yule's K, TTR) are length-dependent folklore when used alone.
- **Feature 2 ("Write like them") is the risky one and cannot be made provably identity-free** — style/content disentanglement is explicitly an open problem in the literature. You can build strong *engineering* guardrails (NER stripping, numeral/date/URL removal, n-gram overlap gates, never letting raw reference text reach the profile) and *measure* leakage, but you cannot assert mathematical non-leakage. Legally, "manner of writing" is not protected by copyright in the US, EU or Russia (idea/expression; ГК РФ ст. 1259(5)), but naming a living author triggers passing-off / right-of-publicity / unfair-competition and endorsement-confusion risk.
- **For a self-hosted AGPL product with Russian as primary language, licensing and language coverage are the binding constraints**: LUAR is Apache-2.0 but English-only; Wegmann Style-Embeddings have no stated weights license and are English-only; Russian NLP tooling (Natasha/Slovnet MIT, DeepPavlov Apache-2.0, Stanza Apache-2.0, spaCy MIT, UDPipe MPL/CC) is AGPL-compatible and should carry the interpretable feature layer.

## Key Findings

### Section 1 — Measuring style computationally

**Established.** Since Mosteller & Wallace's Federalist Papers study, function-word frequencies are the single most-cited reliable, topic-independent authorship marker; Stamatatos's 2009 survey and multiple systematic reviews confirm function words, character n-grams and punctuation as the most discriminative and cross-domain-robust features. A 2023 systematic review ("Enhancing Representation Generalization in Authorship Identification") rates character n-grams and function words four-star ("discriminative, frequent, widely dispersed, content independent"), punctuation and syntax three-star. Burrows's Delta on most-frequent-words is the classical "gold standard" baseline.

**Contested.** Whether function words beat content words. A 2025 PLOS One paper ("Attributing authorship via the perplexity of authorial language models") challenges the orthodoxy, finding content words — especially nouns — carry more authorial information than function words when using authorial language models. This is a genuine disagreement: classical stylometry privileges function words *because* they are topic-independent (safer for cross-topic transfer), while LLM-based attribution extracts more raw signal from content words (riskier for identity leakage — directly relevant to Feature 2).

**Folklore / weak.** Vocabulary-richness measures (Yule's K, Honoré's R, type-token ratio, hapax ratio) are repeatedly flagged as unreliable because they are strongly length-dependent (Tweedie & Baayen 1998; Stamatatos survey: "considered unreliable to be used alone"). Mendenhall word-length curves are of historical interest only. These should be used only with length-normalisation or as weak supporting features.

**Russian transfer — what breaks and what carries.** Russian is morphologically rich and free-word-order, so several English-derived features must be reformulated:
- *Invalid / weakened for Russian*: rigid word-order n-grams as syntactic proxies; article frequency (Russian has no articles — a top-5 English discriminator disappears); raw fixed word-order emphasis features.
- *Russian-specific signal*: particles (же, ли, бы, вот, ведь), which function like Japanese particles shown to be strong AA markers; aspectual pairs (perfective/imperfective verb choice); case-government patterns; word-order-for-emphasis (theme-rheme / actual division), which carries pragmatic signal precisely because order is free; rich morphological features (animacy, gender, diminutives). Per Okulska et al., StyloMetrix (arXiv:2309.12810; NASK-NLP): "A total of 95 metrics are supported for the Russian language. The metrics describe lexical forms, parts of speech, syntactic forms, and verb forms" — including morphological features animacy, gender, and diminutive (MDPI *Information* 15(6):340, 2024). **Important caveat**: the StyloMetrix GitHub README warns that "support for Russian and Ukrainian languages will no longer be available," so do not build a hard dependency on it — replicate its Russian metrics on your own stack (Slovnet/Stanza/spaCy-ru). Russian "author's invariant" work (Morozov/Fomenko tradition) uses the percentage of service words (conjunctions, prepositions, particles — ~55 words total).

**Minimum corpus size (numbers with sources).**
- Maciej Eder, "Does size matter? Authorship attribution, small samples, big problem," *Digital Scholarship in the Humanities* 30(2):167–182 (2015): "the minimal sample length varied from 2,500 words (Latin prose) to 5,000 or so words (in most cases, including English, German, Polish, and Hungarian novels)"; and pointedly, "samples shorter than 5000 words provide a poor 'guessing'… Below the size of 3000 words, the obtained results are simply disastrous." Eder's short-samples follow-up lowers the floor to ~2,000 words *only for texts with a clear authorial signal*.
- Russian-specific: "StylometryRy" (Russian) required a minimum of 5,500 words for classification; the author's-invariant service-word method is effective only for texts of 1,000–2,000 words and up.
- Function Word Adjacency Networks (Segarra et al.): for 40,000-word profiles, binary attribution accuracy 0.985; for 80,000-word profiles among 10 candidates, 0.935; three authors with 70,000-word profiles average 0.943. "Little benefit beyond 100,000 words."
- Short text: ALMs reach 70% macro-accuracy with 40 tokens on Blogs50 but need 400 tokens on CCAT50 — i.e., the floor is domain-dependent. SMS/tweet studies report low accuracy (20–68%) for very short texts with many authors.
- **Honest gap**: there is no published minimum-corpus figure specifically for Russian short-form social posts (200–4000 chars). We must not extrapolate silently — this is a gap.

**Learned representations vs interpretable vectors.** Learned contrastive AV models (LUAR, STAR, Style Embeddings) materially outperform interpretable feature vectors on large-scale cross-domain attribution. Per Huertas-Tato et al., "Understanding writing style in social media…" (*Knowledge-Based Systems* 296:111867, 2024; arXiv:2310.11081), STAR was "trained on a large corpus derived from public sources of 4.5·10^6 authored texts involving 70k heterogeneous authors," and "using a support base of 8 documents of 512 tokens, we can discern authors from sets of up to 1616 authors with at least 80% accuracy" (weights at HuggingFace AIDA-UPM/star). LUAR does well on zero-shot cross-domain transfer (Reddit/Amazon/fanfiction). PAN 2022 showed a naïve character-n-gram cosine baseline beat many neural submissions on varied domains/lengths — so learned models are not universally better. **Licences and Russian coverage (the binding constraints for this product):**
- **LUAR** (rrivera1849/LUAR-MUD, LUAR-CRUD): Apache-2.0 for both weights and code ("LUAR is distributed under the terms of the Apache License (Version 2.0)"); base sentence-transformers/paraphrase-distilroberta-base-v1; ~82.5M params; **English-only** (language: en declared).
- **Wegmann Style-Embeddings** (AnnaWegmann/Style-Embedding): base roberta-base (~125M); **no license stated on the weights** (a real risk for shipping under AGPL — contact the author before relying on it); English-only (Reddit).
- **STEL** (Wegmann & Nguyen, EMNLP 2021): an evaluation *framework/benchmark*, not a model; English-only; gated data (Yahoo permission required for the formality set).
- **STAR** (AIDA-UPM/star): RoBERTa-large based; social-media English.
- **Russian coverage**: no English style-embedding model covers Russian. The only learned style/authorship embeddings covering Russian are multilingual: **mStyleDistance** (StyleDistance/mstyledistance, 9 languages incl. Russian) and **MSR** (Kim et al. 2025, arXiv:2509.16531, 36 languages incl. Russian, trained on 4.5M authors; reports outperforming monolingual baselines in 21 of 22 non-English languages, +4.85% average Recall@8). Their licences must be verified before AGPL deployment (not confirmed in this research).
- Russian AA is dominated by classical ML: Romanov/Fedotova et al. (*Future Internet* 13(1):3, 2021) report ~96% SVM accuracy on *long literary* Russian texts, but short social-media Russian is much harder — in RUSProfiling the best system (Markov et al., 2017) reached only ~68.25% on binary classification of Russian tweets, and Fedotova et al. (*Future Internet* 14(1):4, 2022) found short comments notably harder than literary texts.

### Section 2 — LLM-based voice extraction

**Established prompting architectures.** Four candidate designs map onto known summarisation patterns:
- *Single-pass over concatenated samples* ("stuffing"): coherent but fails past the context window and dilutes attention over long inputs.
- *Map-reduce* (per-sample observation, then synthesis): the standard scalable pattern; parallelizable, debuggable, handles corpora exceeding the window. Documented failure modes: duplicate observations across chunks (the reduce step must dedupe), and cost (one call per chunk).
- *Iterative refinement with a critic*: improves quality but adds cost/latency.
- *Hybrid (deterministic metrics first, handed to the model as evidence)*: strongly supported by both the stylometry literature and recent grounded-evaluation work (TinyStyler grounds personalization evaluation in LUAR + function-word stylometry + LLM-judge). This is the recommended architecture — it converts vague adjectives into evidenced observations.

**Sample selection/chunking.** Eder's "bags of words" finding — randomly excerpted samples yield better attribution than sequential passages — supports diversity sampling over contiguous chunking. Even sampling across content types is what HubSpot/Writer recommend operationally (single content type per voice performs better in Writer's own docs). Recency weighting is recommended by practitioner sources (HubSpot's help center advises using newer samples that reflect where the voice is headed) — but this is a blog/help-center claim, not peer-reviewed.

**Making the model report observations-with-evidence, not flattery.** The core technique is to force each claim to cite a verbatim span and a computed metric ("mean sentence length 12.3 words, σ 6.1; 43% of posts open with an imperative") rather than adjectives ("engaging and professional"). Hand the model the deterministic metrics as ground truth (the hybrid design). Use paired good/bad examples and annotations (the "we do / we don't" pattern used by Writer's and Claude brand-voice skills). This is largely practitioner best-practice (blogs and product docs), not peer-reviewed prompt research — flagged as such.

**Structured output & schema repair (current best practice).** Constrained/guided decoding (Outlines, XGrammar, vLLM structured_outputs, provider-native JSON-schema modes) enforces schema validity at token-generation time — the strongest guarantee. Fallbacks: json_repair library for minor malformations; validate-and-retry loops that feed the schema violation back to the model; XSD/Pydantic/Zod as the contract. Direct prompting for JSON is the weakest (80–95% valid). For a voice profile, define a Pydantic/JSON-schema contract with enums for categorical fields (formality level, emoji policy) and validate server-side.

**Serialisation for injection.** Evidence points to a *combination*: structured fields (for deterministic control and UI), a short prose description, and few-shot exemplars. Writer's own documentation states voices created from examples "consistently perform better than manually written descriptions." Practitioner consensus (Atom Writer, Glean, Claude skill authors) is that concrete annotated examples teach voice better than adjective lists, and that a focused 2–3 page snapshot beats a 40-page bible. The universal reported failure mode across shipped tools is *voice reversion / drift*: the profile is applied as a prompt prefix and the model's defaults reassert over long generations (Jasper, Copy.ai, Writer users all report this). Mitigation: re-inject voice at each chunk boundary rather than once.

### Section 3 — Separating style from content and identity (Feature 2 crux)

**Established.** Text style transfer is evaluated on three axes: style-transfer strength, content preservation, and fluency (Jin et al. survey; Mir et al.). Disentanglement approaches (VAEs, adversarial, β-VAE-style) exist but a large 2025 meta-evaluation ("Mind the Style Gap") finds content-preservation metrics are systematically confounded — widely used metrics correlate with human judgment "despite being deemed unsuitable" because they don't abstract away style change. Authorship obfuscation (the inverse task) is a live PAN research area; "Keep It Private" (2024) uses LUAR and character-n-gram cosine as adversaries to measure de-identification.

**Contested / open.** Whether style and content *can* be disentangled at all. Fast Forward Labs and multiple ACL papers state plainly there is "open debate about whether it's possible to actually decouple style from content in free text," with growing consensus that "style is woven into the content" and separability is attribute-dependent. **This is the single most important honest finding for Feature 2: you cannot claim provable content-independence, because the field does not have it.**

**Implementable identity-barrier pipeline (assessment of your proposed controls).**
- *NER stripping* (names, orgs, locations): necessary but insufficient — NER recall is imperfect (Russian RuBERT-general NER F1 ≈ 0.91 on test), so ~9% of entities leak. Use Natasha/Slovnet + DeepPavlov ensemble to raise recall.
- *Numeral/date removal*: catches figures/statistics; regex + Natasha's date extractors.
- *URL/handle removal*: high-precision regex; low failure rate.
- *N-gram overlap guards between source and output*: essential — reject any output sharing (say) a 4-gram+ with the reference. This is the same mechanism obfuscation researchers use as an adversary.
- *Never letting raw reference text reach the profile*: the strongest single control — the profile should contain only abstracted, quantified observations (metrics + category labels), never verbatim spans, when in "Write like them" mode. (Note this conflicts with Feature 1's evidence-with-quotes approach — Feature 2 must strip quotes.)

**What you are missing.**
- *Paraphrase/round-trip defence*: even without shared n-grams, distinctive facts/opinions can leak semantically. Add a semantic-similarity gate (embed source claims, reject outputs that reproduce them).
- *Topic/entity denylist derived from the source*: extract the source's named entities and salient claims and add them as *negative* constraints to generation.
- *Rare-word / neologism filter*: an author's coined terms are identity markers; filter low-frequency content words.
- *Human review gate* before activation (already in your design — keep it).

**Measuring leakage (concrete tests).**
- *Entity-leakage rate*: run NER on generated output; assert zero source entities appear. Target: 0.
- *N-gram overlap*: max source-output shared n-gram length below threshold (e.g., no shared 4-gram); report longest common subsequence.
- *Content-preservation-reversed*: use a content-similarity metric (SBERT cosine, BLEU/ROUGE) between source and output — you want these *low* (opposite of style-transfer's goal).
- *Authorship-verification adversary*: run an AV model (LUAR for English; a Russian classical-ML AV or mStyleDistance/MSR for Russian) on (source, output) pairs — the target is elevated *style* similarity but content similarity no higher than chance; more practically, test that the output cannot be attributed to the source author on content-controlled features.
- *STEL-or-content style probe*: whether style is captured without content (English only today).

**Prior art in a shipped product with an explicit identity barrier.** Weak/absent. The shipped brand-voice tools (Writer, Jasper, Copy.ai, HubSpot) are all built around *your own* content and explicitly do not offer a bounded "imitate a named author" path. Jasper crawls a URL you own; HubSpot analyses your uploads. No mainstream product ships a "write like <named living person>" feature with a documented identity barrier — likely because of the legal exposure in Section 5. This is a gap in prior art: there is no reference implementation to copy, which raises your design and legal burden.

### Section 4 — Validating that generated text hits the voice

**Established methods.**
- *Holdout design*: train the profile on part of the corpus, validate generated text against a held-out part. This is the most defensible protocol.
- *Automatic style metrics*: function-word/punctuation/sentence-length distribution distance between generated text and the author's holdout (interpretable, language-portable, works for Russian).
- *Authorship-verification model as judge*: embed generated text and author holdout with an AV model (LUAR for English) and measure cosine similarity; TinyStyler establishes grounding personalization evaluation in AV theory.
- *LLM-as-judge*: flexible but biased.

**LLM-as-judge known biases (with mitigations).** Position bias (verdict flips 10–30% when order swapped — mitigate by randomising/averaging both orders); verbosity bias (favours longer outputs — penalise length, separate correctness from style); self-preference/self-enhancement bias — traced to Panickssery, Bowman & Feng, "LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations" (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2404.13076), which establishes self-recognition→self-preference, and Wataoka et al. (arXiv:2410.21819), who found "GPT-4, in particular, exhibits a high self-preference bias" (score 0.520, highest of 8 LLMs) because "LLM evaluators assigned higher evaluations to texts with lower perplexity" (mitigate by using a judge from a different model family than the generator). A 2025 IJCNLP systematic study (Shi et al., ~150,000 instances, 15 judges) found position bias varies by judge/task and that quality-gap, not length, is the dominant predictor. Cross-lingual degradation: judge reliability drops in lower-resource languages — **material for a Russian-primary product**; validate the judge specifically on Russian. Best practice: calibrate the judge against a 100–500-example human-labelled set for your domain and language before trusting it.

**Defensible activation threshold.** No single published number gates a "voice profile." A defensible composite: (1) the generated text's stylometric feature vector falls within the author's within-corpus variance (e.g., Mahalanobis distance to the holdout centroid below the 95th percentile of the author's own inter-sample distances); (2) an AV model scores generated-vs-holdout similarity above the score for generated-vs-other-authors (relative, not absolute); (3) LLM-judge (different family, randomised order, Russian-validated) agreement above the human-calibrated threshold; (4) for Feature 2, all leakage gates pass (zero entity leakage, no shared 4-gram, low content similarity). Gate on the *conjunction*. This is a reasoned recommendation, not a literature-blessed constant — flagged.

**Drift and multilingual authors.** Authors' style changes over time (documented in style-change-detection PAN tasks and temporal/clinical studies). Recommendation: version profiles, timestamp samples, weight recent samples, and re-validate periodically against fresh holdout. For bilingual authors, build *separate* profiles per language — cross-linguistic interference is real (the Russian-French bilingual study found demonstrative/article interference), and function-word inventories are language-specific, so a single merged profile is invalid.

### Section 5 — Legal and ethical boundaries

**Copyright: style vs the underlying texts (established, and consistent across jurisdictions).**
- *US*: idea/expression dichotomy (Baker v. Selden lineage; Feist requires originality of expression). Style/manner is unprotectable; specific expression is. AI copyright litigation over training is unsettled but concerns *copying works*, not *learning manner*.
- *EU*: InfoSoc Directive protects the author's own intellectual creation (expression); DSM Directive Art. 4 text-and-data-mining exception applies unless the rightholder has opted out (machine-readable reservation) — relevant if you mine an author's texts. Ideas/style are not protected.
- *Russia*: **ГК РФ ст. 1259(5) is directly on point** — "авторские права не распространяются на идеи, концепции, принципы, методы, процессы, системы, способы, решения технических, организационных или иных задач, открытия, факты, языки программирования" (copyright does not extend to ideas, concepts, principles, methods, processes, systems, ways, solutions, discoveries, facts, programming languages). Russian doctrine: copyright protects *form*, not *content*; commentary explicitly notes методики обучения (teaching methods) and шахматная партия (a chess game) are unprotected. So *learning a manner* is lawful in Russia; *reproducing a protected work* (or a recognisable original part — ст. 1259(7) protects original parts) is not. ст. 1274 permits limited free use with attribution (citation), not wholesale reproduction.

**The line.** "Learning a manner" (extracting quantified style features, abstracted patterns) sits safely on the unprotected side in all three jurisdictions. "Reproducing a work" begins when output reproduces original expression — recognisable phrases, structure, or substantial parts. Your n-gram-overlap gate is, in effect, the copyright-safety mechanism.

**Personality rights, passing-off, unfair competition, endorsement confusion.**
- *US*: right of publicity + Lanham Act §43(a) false endorsement. **Midler v. Ford (9th Cir. 1988)**: deliberate imitation of a distinctive, widely-known voice to sell a product is a tort even without using the name or actual recording ("when a distinctive voice of a professional singer is widely known and is deliberately imitated in order to sell a product, the sellers have appropriated what is not theirs"). **Waits v. Frito-Lay, Inc., 978 F.2d 1093 (9th Cir. 1992)**: affirmed; per the opinion, "the jury found in Waits' favor, awarding him $375,000 compensatory damages and $2 million punitive damages for voice misappropriation, and $100,000 damages for violation of the Lanham Act." The $375k compensatory broke down as $100k fair-market value of services, $200k injury to peace/happiness/feelings, and $75k injury to goodwill; the Lanham Act award was vacated as duplicative, leaving ~$2,475,000. These are *voice* (audio) cases but the principle — deliberate imitation of a distinctive identity marker for commercial gain, causing endorsement confusion — is the analogy that threatens a "write like <named living person>" feature. Chaplin v. Amador extended protection to imitated persona/style used to deceive the public.
- *Russia*: ст. 152.1 ГК РФ protects a citizen's *image* (изображение) — publication/use requires consent (Plenum guidance: mere public availability of an image does not authorise reuse); закон о защите конкуренции ст. 14.x prohibits недобросовестная конкуренция including creating confusion; закон о рекламе restricts misleading advertising. ст. 19 protects the citizen's name. There is no bespoke Russian "right of publicity," but name/image/anti-unfair-competition provisions cover the endorsement-confusion risk.

**GDPR & EU AI Act.**
- *GDPR*: if the reference corpus contains personal data (it usually does — a named author's posts are personal data), you need a lawful basis, transparency, and often a DPIA; an imitation of a real person "almost always processes personal data."
- *ФЗ-152 (Russia)*: personal-data processing of the reference author requires a lawful basis/consent under Russian law; the corpus is personal data.
- *EU AI Act Art. 50*: transparency obligations apply from **2 August 2026** (with a grace period to 2 December 2026 for the machine-readable marking of content from generative systems already placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026, per the Digital Omnibus, provisional/pending formal adoption; Commission guidelines adopted 20 July 2026). Providers must machine-readably mark AI-generated content; deployers must label deepfakes and AI-generated text published on matters of public interest. Penalties up to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover (Art. 99). Your generated content is in scope for marking; if you offer "write like a named person" you edge toward deepfake-adjacent territory.

**What comparable products do.** Brand-voice tools disclose that the voice is trained on *your* content and confine imitation to the user's own material; none offer a public-figure imitation path — an implicit industry answer to the legal risk. This is inferred from product docs, not a stated policy.

**Recommended framing (decision).** Frame Feature 2 as "**style inspiration**," not "write as/like <Name>." Concretely: (a) default to the user's own voice (Feature 1); (b) for Feature 2, do not use the named author's name in outputs or in marketing of those outputs; (c) present the result as an abstracted, editable *style profile*, never as "content in X's voice"; (d) require the user to attest they have rights to the supplied texts; (e) show the identity-barrier results (leakage report) to the user; (f) mark outputs as AI-generated (AI Act); (g) obtain a lawful basis under GDPR/ФЗ-152 for the reference corpus, or restrict Feature 2 to public-domain / deceased / consenting authors. This keeps you on the unprotected "manner" side of copyright and reduces endorsement-confusion exposure.

## Recommendations

### 1. Recommended pipeline for Feature 1 ("My voice") — implementable
**Architecture: hybrid deterministic-metrics-then-map-reduce-LLM.**
1. **Ingest & segment** (Natasha/Razdel for Russian; per-document).
2. **Deterministic metric layer** (compute first, hand to LLM as evidence):
   - Function-word AND particle frequencies (же/ли/бы/вот/ведь etc.); punctuation-rate profile; sentence-length mean + variance + distribution shape; paragraph structure; POS ratios and nominalisation-vs-verb ratio (Slovnet/Stanza/spaCy-ru); aspectual and case-government features; discourse markers/hedging; emoji/hashtag rates; CTA patterns; character n-grams. Vocabulary richness only length-normalised, as weak features.
3. **Map**: per-sample, LLM emits schema-constrained observations, each citing a verbatim span + the relevant metric.
4. **Reduce**: synthesise into a Pydantic/JSON-schema voice profile (structured fields + short prose + 3–5 annotated exemplars + guardrails), deduping across chunks.
5. **Structured output** via constrained decoding + json_repair fallback + validate-retry.
6. **Human review/edit/approve** → store versioned profile.
7. **Injection**: combine structured fields + prose + exemplars; **re-inject at each generation chunk boundary** to fight drift.

**Feature list (the reliable ones):** function words/particles, punctuation habits, sentence-length distribution+variance, POS/dependency profiles, nominalisation-vs-verb ratio, discourse markers/hedging, aspect, case-government, word-order-for-emphasis, character n-grams, emoji/hashtag policy, CTA habits. **De-emphasise** raw TTR/Yule's K/Honoré's R (length-dependent).

**Sample-size floor:** require **minimum ~2,000 words total** across samples for a provisional profile; **flag profiles below ~5,000 words as low-confidence** (Eder 2015: below 3,000 words results are "simply disastrous"). For short social posts, require **more items** (aim ≥15–25 posts) to compensate for per-item brevity; below this, warn the user. State to the user that no Russian-short-form-specific floor is published.

**Prompting architecture named:** **hybrid metrics-grounded map-reduce with schema-constrained output and a critic pass** on the reduce step.

### 2. Recommended pipeline for Feature 2 ("Write like them") — with identity barrier + proof test
**Pipeline:**
1. **Rights attestation** gate (user confirms rights; restrict to public-domain/deceased/consenting authors by default).
2. **Pre-processing scrubber on reference text** (before anything reaches the model): NER stripping (Natasha+DeepPavlov ensemble), numeral/date removal, URL/handle removal, rare-word/neologism filter.
3. **Metrics-only profile**: compute the *same deterministic style metrics as Feature 1* but **emit NO verbatim quotes** — the profile contains only quantified/abstracted style patterns and category labels. Raw reference text never reaches the profile or generation prompt.
4. **Negative-constraint layer**: extract source entities + salient claims/opinions; add them as a *denylist* to generation.
5. **Generation** under the abstract style profile only.
6. **Leakage gates (post-generation, must all pass to activate):**
   - Entity-leakage rate = 0 (NER on output vs source entity set);
   - No shared n-gram ≥ 4 tokens with source (report longest common subsequence);
   - Content-similarity (SBERT cosine, ROUGE) source-vs-output *below* threshold;
   - Semantic-claim gate: no source salient claim reproduced.
7. **Human review** of the leakage report before activation.
8. **Framing/labelling**: present as editable "style inspiration," mark output AI-generated (AI Act), do not use the author's name in output.

**The test that proves the barrier works:** Construct a **held-out identity probe**. From author X's corpus, hold out documents containing X's distinctive entities/claims/figures. Generate N outputs under the derived profile on unrelated prompts. Assert, as an automated test: (a) **zero** of X's held-out named entities, dates, or numerals appear in any output; (b) **no shared 4-gram** between any output and any X source document; (c) content-similarity (SBERT) between outputs and X's content-bearing holdout is **statistically indistinguishable from** outputs-vs-random-other-authors (i.e., the profile carries no more of X's *content* than chance) — while the *style* similarity (function-word/AV features) is elevated. Passing (a)+(b)+(c) is the operational proof of "style transferred, identity/content not." **Caveat, stated plainly: this proves absence of *measured* leakage on these axes, not mathematical content-independence, which the literature says is unattainable.**

### 3. Validation protocol with a defensible activation threshold
Use a **holdout design** (train profile on 70–80% of corpus; validate on held-out 20–30%). Activate a profile version only if the **conjunction** holds:
- **Stylometric fit**: generated text's feature vector within the author's own within-corpus variance (Mahalanobis distance to holdout centroid < 95th percentile of the author's inter-sample distances).
- **AV-model agreement**: generated-vs-holdout similarity > generated-vs-other-authors (relative test; LUAR for EN; classical-ML AV or mStyleDistance/MSR for RU).
- **LLM-judge** (different model family from generator; randomised order averaged over both; validated on a 100–500-example Russian human-labelled calibration set): score above the human-calibrated pass mark.
- **Feature 2 only**: all leakage gates pass.
Re-validate on drift: version profiles, timestamp/weight recent samples, re-run quarterly or on N new documents. Separate profiles per language for bilingual authors.
**Threshold honesty:** these are reasoned composite gates, not a literature constant; calibrate the numeric cut-offs on your own held-out data during a pilot.

### 4. Legal risk summary + recommended framing
- **Copyright**: learning a manner is lawful (US idea/expression; EU expression-only + DSM Art. 4 TDM opt-out; **RU ГК РФ ст. 1259(5)**). Reproducing protected expression is not — the n-gram-overlap gate is your copyright safety mechanism. Respect TDM opt-outs when mining EU authors.
- **Personality/endorsement**: **Midler** and **Waits** make deliberate imitation of a distinctive identity marker for commercial gain, causing endorsement confusion, actionable in the US (right of publicity + Lanham §43(a); Waits recovered ~$2.475M); RU covers this via ст. 152.1 (image), ст. 19 (name), закон о защите конкуренции ст. 14.x (unfair competition/confusion), закон о рекламе.
- **Data protection**: reference corpora are personal data — GDPR (lawful basis, DPIA) and **ФЗ-152** (lawful basis/consent) apply.
- **EU AI Act Art. 50** (from 2 Aug 2026; marking grace period to 2 Dec 2026): mark AI-generated content machine-readably; label deepfakes/public-interest AI text; fines to €15M/3%.
- **Recommended framing**: default to the user's own voice; call Feature 2 "style inspiration," never "write as <Name>"; don't surface the author's name in output; require rights attestation; restrict by default to public-domain/deceased/consenting authors; show the leakage report; mark outputs AI-generated; secure a lawful basis for the reference corpus. This keeps the product on the unprotected-manner side and minimises endorsement-confusion and data-protection exposure.

### 5. The three assumptions I am least confident about, and the cheapest experiment to settle each
1. **That ~2,000 words (or ≥15–25 short posts) is a sufficient corpus floor for a stable Russian short-form voice profile.** No published Russian-short-form figure exists. *Cheapest experiment*: an ablation on a held-out Russian Telegram/social corpus — build profiles from 500/1,000/2,000/5,000 words and N=5/15/30 posts, measure AV-model and stylometric stability of the resulting generations; find the knee of the curve.
2. **That the identity-barrier gates (NER + n-gram + content-similarity) actually stop leakage in practice for Russian.** Russian NER recall is ~0.91, so entities can leak. *Cheapest experiment*: red-team the barrier — derive profiles from 10 well-known Russian authors, generate 100 outputs each, and have annotators (or an AV adversary) attempt to identify the source author and spot leaked facts/entities; measure the true leakage rate against the automated gates.
3. **That LLM-as-judge is reliable enough for Russian voice-matching to gate activation.** Cross-lingual judge degradation is documented. *Cheapest experiment*: assemble a 200-pair Russian human-labelled set (matches/mismatches), run your candidate judge (and a different-family judge) with randomised order, and measure agreement with humans; if agreement is poor, fall back to stylometric + AV gates only for Russian.

## Caveats
- **Style/content disentanglement is an open research problem**; Feature 2 cannot be marketed as provably identity-free. All "proof" is absence-of-measured-leakage, not mathematical guarantee.
- **The function-word-vs-content-word question is genuinely contested** (classical stylometry vs 2025 authorial-LM findings); we recommend function words/particles for *safety* (topic-independence), accepting they may capture less raw signal.
- **No published minimum-corpus size exists for Russian short-form social posts** — our floor is extrapolated from literary/English work (Eder 2015) and must be validated (Experiment 1).
- **The best learned style models (LUAR, Style-Embeddings, STAR) are English-only**; Russian coverage requires multilingual models (mStyleDistance, MSR) whose licences must be verified for AGPL compatibility, or reliance on the interpretable feature layer (Natasha/Slovnet MIT, DeepPavlov/Stanza Apache-2.0, spaCy MIT — all AGPL-compatible). Note also that StyloMetrix has announced it will drop Russian/Ukrainian support, so replicate its Russian metrics rather than depend on it.
- **Product-benchmark details from blogs/review sites/vendor pages are labelled as such**; primary help-center facts (HubSpot word minimums, Writer's 8-box/300-word input, Jasper URL-crawl) are more reliable. Typefully/Taplio and Russian-market tools are genuine gaps where authoritative docs were not found.
- **Legal analysis is not legal advice**; the AI-copyright-training litigation is unsettled, and the EU AI Act Art. 50 implementation (guidelines, Code of Practice, Digital Omnibus grace period) was still settling as of mid-2026 — verify the exact obligations with counsel before launch.
- **LLM-judge biases (position, verbosity, self-preference, cross-lingual)** mean any judge must be human-calibrated on Russian before it gates anything.
